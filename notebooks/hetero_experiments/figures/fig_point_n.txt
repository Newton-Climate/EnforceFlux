Two panels for point-sensor networks against the number of sensors n from 1 to 4, over the 288 point-sensor inversions. The left panel plots two quantities as a percentage of the true flux. Mean absolute error, the actual error, falls from 19 percent at one sensor to 13, 12 and 11 percent at two, three and four, so it flattens after the second sensor. The uncertainty the retrieval reports lies above the actual error at every n and falls steadily, from 35 to 29 to 24 to 21 percent. The right panel gives the reduction contributed by each additional sensor in percentage points. The second sensor reduces actual error by 5.9 points, but the third and fourth contribute only 0.8 and 1.0 points. Reported uncertainty instead keeps falling by 6.7, 4.5 and 3.5 points. Beyond the second sensor the network reports steadily improving precision while its actual accuracy has stopped improving, because the limiting error is the representativeness of the sample and that error is common to every sensor on the fence rather than independent between them.
